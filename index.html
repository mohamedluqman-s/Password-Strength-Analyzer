Cyber Defense Best Practices

Password Security & Hygiene

Core principles recommended by cybersecurity standards (NIST SP 800-63B, OWASP, and CISA)

Adopt Multi-Word Passphrases

Length provides exponential defense against brute-force attacks compared to complex short words. Passphrases composed of 4 or 5 randomly chosen words (e.g. correct-horse-battery-staple) are easy to remember yet astronomically difficult to crack.

Use a Trusted Password Manager

Humans cannot safely remember dozens of unique, 16+ character high-entropy passwords. Modern password managers (such as Bitwarden, 1Password, or KeePass) store credentials inside AES-256 encrypted vaults.

Enforce Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Even if a password is compromised in a breach or phishing campaign, Hardware Security Keys (FIDO2 / WebAuthn) and Authenticator apps (TOTP) stop 99.9% of automated account takeover attempts.

Eliminate Password Reuse

Credential Stuffing is an automated attack where cybercriminals take billions of username/password pairs leaked from one breached site and test them across banking, email, and social networks. Never reuse passwords across sites.

Avoid Predictable Leetspeak

Replacing 'e' with '3' or 'a' with '@' (e.g. P@ssw0rd1!) no longer deceives modern attack software. Modern password cracking tools (like Hashcat and John the Ripper) automatically evaluate leetspeak permutation rules in microseconds.

Zero-Trust Local Audit Architecture

Never test actual high-value corporate master passwords on random online websites. This application is architected with a strict zero-trust model: all checks execute inside your browser's V8 engine with zero network connectivity.
